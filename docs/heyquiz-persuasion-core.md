# HeyQuiz persuasion core

Distilled from Perry’s supplied Persuasion Quiz Studio V1.1 instructions, seven supporting text guides, and playbook, with his current emphasis on questions as unobtrusive persuasive copy. This is a proposed product/writing specification, not an installed skill or implemented app feature. The separate DOCX staff guide was not needed for this distillation and has not been reviewed.

## Purpose

Help a suitable prospect discover what they want, understand the relevant benefits, and see why the recommended next step fits. The questions and answer choices do part of the selling while remaining easy, natural, and useful to answer.

## Minimal creator brief

1. What are we offering, and what action should a suitable person take?
2. Who is answering, and what do they already know or want?
3. Which actual benefits can the offer deliver, and what supports them?
4. What changes the recommendation or makes someone a poor fit?
5. What commonly makes people hesitate?

Use supplied product facts and research. Ask only for missing information that changes the draft. Keep unknown claims out of publishable copy.

## The writing method

Plan the useful result and offer first. Then write the shortest natural conversation that prepares it.

- Open with one or two easy, relevant questions about the person’s situation.
- Introduce desirable outcomes through short answer choices. Ask which matters most rather than asking for agreement with a sales claim.
- Use three or four distinct, concrete choices when appropriate. Similar length and grammar make them easy to compare.
- Mix question roles naturally: context, priority, fit, desired benefit, concern. Do not force every quiz through a fixed checklist or repeat the same benefit question.
- Include an unsure, none, or skip path where the choices might not fit.
- Use the selected priority later. An answer may choose a product, segment, useful advice, explanation, proof, or objection response. A persuasion-only question is allowed when it introduces a distinct supported benefit and earns the extra step.
- Keep preferences separate from factual assessment scores. A desired benefit does not prove a problem or lack of ability.
- Remove jargon, leading praise for the product, repeated promises, unnecessary typing, and questions whose main effect is friction.

Aim for a short first draft, often 5–8 questions; actual product-fit needs determine length. Do not force extra questions to meet a quota.

## Example: hypothetical baking course

Assume the actual course teaches planning, troubleshooting, and making a presentable loaf.

Weak: “Which amazing benefit of our revolutionary course excites you most?”

Better: “What would make your next bake feel like a win?”

- A loaf you’re proud to put on the table.
- Fewer ingredients wasted on disappointing attempts.
- A baking routine that fits your day.
- I’m still figuring that out.

The choices introduce pride, less waste, and convenience while learning a priority. They do not establish that the respondent has a problem or guarantee that the course will achieve those outcomes.

A later concern question could ask: “What would you want to know before trying a new baking course?” Options might cover lesson length, equipment required, suitability for beginners, or no questions yet. Show only answers relevant to the actual offer.

## Smooth transitions

Use a transition only when it improves understanding. Avoid a sales pitch after every answer. Motion should be brief, preserve focus and answers, and respect reduced-motion preferences. Never use fake analysis or forced waiting to make results appear more sophisticated.

Question → acknowledgment grounded in the answer → useful result → relevant offer.

Example result bridge for the schedule choice:

“You chose a routine that fits your day. Start by deciding when you want to bake, then work backward through the recipe’s timing. The course’s planning lesson can help you put that schedule together.”

Lead capture should continue the same promise. If an actual personalized plan will be delivered, “Want a copy of your baking plan?” is more natural than an unrelated newsletter interruption. Make required capture clear before entry; support optional capture and separate result delivery from marketing permission. Do not promise email delivery until it is connected.

The result should provide value without requiring a purchase. Explain the recommendation with actual answers and product facts, then present one clear action with accurate terms. Support unsure and no-fit outcomes.

## What HeyQuiz should store behind the interface

Keep the authoring interface simple. The application can maintain the necessary structure automatically:

- Stable question and answer IDs.
- One main question purpose and optional benefit introduced.
- Answer effects: fit rules, category contributions, segment tags, recommendation inputs, result-copy selections.
- Neutral fallback and changed-answer behavior.
- Separate contact request and marketing-permission state.
- Source facts for claims that need support.

Show optional “Why this question?” and advanced rule controls rather than requiring a creator to complete a long job card for every field. Preserve full advanced builder controls independently of this simplified writing workflow.

## Draft review

Ask five questions:

1. Is this pleasant and easy to answer?
2. Does each screen learn something useful or introduce a distinct relevant benefit?
3. Are the choices honest, concrete, and free of forced agreement?
4. Does the result visibly use the answers and explain the offer’s fit?
5. Can a suitable buyer take the next step without an abrupt sales or contact gate?

Then validate routing, calculations, fallback paths, mobile/keyboard behavior, and connected destinations mechanically. Keep campaign research, detailed QA, and experiment plans available when needed; do not require seven separate document handoffs for every draft.

## Measurement

Treat persuasion as a testable hypothesis. Compare benefit-rich and plain wording of the same preference while holding routing, result rules, offer, and follow-up constant. Track exits by question, lead-capture abandonment, offer clicks, paid buyers, and contribution per assigned visitor. Completion alone is not evidence of increased sales.

## Packaging recommendation

Use one concise generation guide—the core above—with the seven original modules as optional references for research, complex builds, follow-up, and experimentation. Keep the original archive intact. Simplify the creator’s work without removing the platform’s advanced capabilities.
